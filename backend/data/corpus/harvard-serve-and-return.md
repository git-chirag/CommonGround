Serve and return describes responsive, back-and-forth exchanges between a young child and a caring adult. When a baby or young child babbles, gestures or cries and an adult responds with eye contact, words or a hug, this exchange helps build and strengthen connections in the child's brain that support communication and social skills.

The Center describes these interactions as an important part of a child's social environment and a foundation for later, more complex abilities. Attentive and responsive caregivers create an environment rich in these exchanges, and the page links to a handout of practical steps for serve and return.

The page also says that stress from financial pressure, isolation or chronic health problems can make responsive caregiving harder, and that supports for caregivers matter. Consistently absent or harmful responses to a young child can disrupt development, which is why stable, responsive relationships are emphasised.
