UNICEF explains that a teenager's interest in new experiences, and pushing against limits set by adults, is a natural part of growing up and developing independence, even though it can be a worrying time for parents.

According to the page, teens tend to reason carefully about risk when things are calm, but in emotionally charged situations with friends they are more likely to act on impulse. The kinds of risks teens take depend more on their surroundings than on traits like gender or age.

To help keep teens safe, the page suggests reasonable supervision and offering yourself as a partner in your teen's safety: making safety something you work on together, not a list of rules. It recommends centring expectations on keeping safe rather than on rules or morals, which teens can find arbitrary.

Parents who are worried are encouraged to say so directly to their teen and then talk through the teen's own plans for staying safe. Parents can make it clear that if a teen or their friends ever feel unsafe, they can reach out for help and will not be made sorry for asking. Planning in advance how a teen will handle a risky moment at a party or with friends helps, because thinking it through beforehand is easier than deciding in the heat of the moment.

The page advises against threatening punishment if a teen gets caught, because it can shift the focus to what they can get away with instead of staying safe. The aim is a young person who can be independent and look after themselves.
