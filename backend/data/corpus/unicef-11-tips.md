Connection with a teenager is the base for supporting their wellbeing and their social and emotional learning. Showing interest in what matters to them, sharing things about yourself, finding shared interests and asking for their opinions and perspectives all help build the relationship. Communication habits formed when children were younger tend to carry into adolescence.

Active listening means being engaged, caring, non-judgmental and empathetic, even when you disagree. A teenager's beliefs or opinions may differ from a parent's. UNICEF advises respecting and valuing their views, which also helps teens respect the parent's views. Listening well helps a young person feel heard, understood and calmer. Brushing off their concerns can leave them feeling defensive, frustrated, alone or hurt.

Practical listening skills include attentive body language such as eye contact and nodding. Open-ended, clarifying questions have no right or wrong answer and help a parent understand what the teen thinks, for example asking them to explain what they mean. Mirroring means restating what you heard and checking that you understood correctly.

Specific, immediate praise can build a teenager's confidence. Validating what a teen is feeling helps them accept their emotions and feel safe expressing them, for example by saying that feeling stressed or angry is understandable.

If a teen finds it hard to talk, a parent can say they are available and ready to listen at any time, without forcing the conversation. Communication is also about sharing good moments, humour and affection, not only difficulties.
